Exposed cutaway

THREE-QUARTER VIEW
PERSPECTIVE · NOT TO SCALE

The near hull peeled back like a halved seed. The interior is a single lit hollow — its whole inner surface a photosynthesizing skin[7] — spanned by the axial core spine and the rotating habitat girdle. At the cut, the six-layer rind reads as a thick banded edge (enlarged in Plate IV).

PLATE I

General arrangement · external profile

SCALE 1 : 600 (1 px ≈ 0.17 m)
STATIONS 0–10 @ 10 m

The whole organism in side elevation, sunward pole to the left. Prolate spheroid main body, 100 m between poles by 50 m beam, with its external organs: light-gathering cap forward, radiator bloom and mining root aft, and the equatorial band of docking sphincters.

PLATE II

Longitudinal section · internal anatomy

SECTION ON CENTERLINE
SCALE 1 : 600

The ship opened on its long axis. A continuous core spine (vascular trunk) runs pole to pole, swelling into the central reservoir-liver; the habitat girdle rings the equator; sealing bulkhead sphincters divide the hollow into survivable compartments; the gut chambers recycle waste aft. Green stipple marks the photosynthetic intima lining the inner wall.

PLATE III

Transverse body sections

SECTIONS A·B·C · COMMON SCALE 1 : 720
LOOKING FORWARD (SUNWARD)

Three cuts across the body, drawn to one scale so their true relative sizes read at a glance. Note the shielding asymmetry at B–B — the water jacket thickens toward the galactic plane[5][6] — and the rotating girdle nested inside the fixed hull. The three diameters are simply the prolate spheroid's width at each station — Ø(x) = 50·√(1 − (x/50)²) m — so A–A at St.1.6 reads ~37 m, C–C at St.7.8 ~41 m, and the B–B equator the full 50 m beam: A < C < B.

PLATE IV

Hull wall · layer detail

DETAIL 1 · SCALE 1 : 43 (1 px ≈ 0.008 m)
VACUUM (LEFT) → CABIN (RIGHT)

A radial core through the 5.5 m wall. The pressure seal is only the ~1 m pneumostat — a stack of hundreds of gas-tight lamellae; the metres of tissue outboard of it are structure, shielding and living skin[1][2][3][4].

PLATE V

Detail studies · organs & systems

DETAILS 2–6 · VARIOUS SCALES

Close-ups of the working parts: how the crew passes through a living wall, how fluid and light are handled, how a wound closes, and what actually gets launched.

Docking sphincterDET. 2

A ring of muscular leaves irises open for docking and clamps airtight on command — the same mechanism that seals a breached compartment.

Vascular nodeDET. 3

Xylem (out) and phloem (in) trunks meet at a valved node. Ring muscles pump by peristalsis — the ship's slow, distributed heartbeat.

Phototropic lensDET. 4

Transparent panels in the sunward cap admit and focus starlight, filtering UV, onto the photosynthetic intima. Bioluminescent organs cover the dark side.

Micrometeoroid self-healDET. 5

Minutes: a fibrin-analogue plug clots the breach against the pressure gradient. Days: stem cells rebuild the layers in order — seal first — and remodel the scar.

The launched seedDET. 6

All you launch: a radiation-hard, desiccated pod of stem cells, an error-corrected genome vault, a starter culture and a nutrient charge. Everything else is grown on arrival.

Drawing conventions

Green = photosynthetic / living tissue · cyan = engineered systems & data · amber = structure, fluid & metabolic heat. Chain-dashed lines are centrelines; dashed lines are hidden edges. The 1.8 m figure recurs for scale.

Scale caveat

Orthographic plates (I–IV) are dimensioned and drawn to the stated scales. In the section views the hull rim is drawn thicker than 1:scale for legibility. Plate 0 is a perspective illustration — read dimensions from the orthographic sheets.

Status

A conceptual design set, not flight hardware. Component technologies are real and mostly demonstrated; the single coordinated organism at this scale is far beyond current synthetic biology. Companion to the GESTALT design dossier.

SOURCES

References & sources

THE PUBLISHED BASIS

The drawing set is dimensioned to the design's real numbers; the living materials it renders — each hull layer, the shielding and the photosynthetic skin — rest on published work, cited here. The prolate-spheroid geometry (the section diameters, Ø(x) = 50·√(1−(x/50)²)) is plane mathematics and needs no source.

  1. Jones, M. et al. (2020). Engineered mycelium composite construction materials. Mater. Des. 187, 108397. — the mycelial matrix layer (chitin scaffold).
  2. Wegst, U. G. K. et al. (2015). Bioinspired structural materials. Nat. Mater. 14, 23–36. — the osteoid (collagen–hydroxyapatite) bone truss.
  3. Gosline, J. M. et al. (1999). The mechanical design of spider silks. J. Exp. Biol. 202, 3295–3303. — silk-analogue fibre in the pneumostat pressure seal.
  4. Dadachova, E. et al. (2007). Ionizing radiation changes the electronic properties of melanin and enhances the growth of melanized fungi. PLoS ONE 2, e457. — the melanized rind.
  5. Zeitlin, C. et al. (2013). Measurements of energetic particle radiation in transit to Mars on the MSL. Science 340, 1080–1084. — the GCR the hull shields against.
  6. Durante, M. & Cucinotta, F. A. (2011). Physical basis of radiation protection in space travel. Rev. Mod. Phys. 83, 1245–1281. — the water-jacket areal-density shielding (thickened toward the galactic plane).
  7. Lasseur, C. et al. (2010). MELiSSA: the European project of a closed life-support system. Gravit. Space Biol. 23, 3–12. — the algal intima (photosynthetic inner skin).
