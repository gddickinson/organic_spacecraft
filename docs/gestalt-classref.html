ATLAS Onus vivum

Freighter Bulk cargo hauler & tanker — the fleet's logistics

The fleet's freight and tanker — a grown keel strung with modular holds like a fish heavy with egg-sacs. Pressurised holds carry biomass and seeds, cryogenic holds carry MEDUSA's ices, open holds carry RADIX's ore pellets; each can be dropped off and resealed. It runs on its own cargo, electrolysing water for reaction mass. This is the logistics that closes the ecosystem: harvesters gather, ATLAS moves, GRAVID grows, habitats consume.

In the fleet: the fleet's logistics spine: the bulk hauler and tanker that moves refined matter between growers and nurseries.

VESPER Vigil viva

Sentinel Relay, navigation & deep-space watch — a constellation

The fleet's eyes and ears, grown not built. A tensioned parabolic dish laced with biomineralised magnetite (magnetotactic bacteria make magnetite for real) gives it radio; grown lens-and-retina 'eyes' and a bioluminescent beacon give it light. Deployed in hundreds and spread across millions of kilometres, the nodes act as one interferometer (~microarcsecond) and a mesh relay — comms, navigation, and an early-warning watch that feeds targets to RADIX and MEDUSA.

In the fleet: the fleet's nervous system at range — a constellation of relays, navigation beacons and deep-space watch.

CHORUS Memoria viva

Archive Cultural memory & knowledge archive — distributed

The fleet's living memory. Where TARDIGRADE backs up the genome, CHORUS backs up the culture — every text, law, language, record and artwork — and keeps it coherent across light-hours and generations. It writes to grown DNA archives (real DNA data storage packs ~an exabyte into a few grams) and reconciles versions across nodes through VESPER's mesh, like an immune memory that never forgets. Its deepest problem isn't storage — grams hold everything — but keeping a civilisation's shared meaning from fragmenting across the distances between its worlds.

In the fleet: the fleet's memory: the distributed, error-corrected archive that stores the canonical genomes and the culture — the master no vessel grows without.

TESTUDO Testudo viva

Guardian Defensive armoured escort & storm shelter

Strictly a shield — it survives hits and dodges, it does not shoot. The fleet's tortoise: a compact, extraordinarily armoured escort that interposes its regrowing carapace between a hazard and something vulnerable — a farm, a liner full of sleepers, a young thin-shelled habitat — or opens a shelter cavity for a crew to ride out a solar storm. NEREUS Abyssus viva

Diver Ice-moon subsurface-ocean explorer

The fleet's oceanographer — and its most fraught member. NEREUS lands on an ice moon (Europa, Enceladus), grows a hot melt-head that bores down through kilometres of crust while its tail refreezes the channel behind it, then swims the black ocean beneath as a pressure-equalized organism — no gas voids, incompressible like a Mariana-trench fish but built for the ~150 MPa of a moon's ocean floor (deeper than any trench on Earth). Bioluminescent, it explores, samples hydrothermal vents, and hunts for the thing the whole program tiptoes around: native alien life.

In the fleet: the fleet's diver — the one class built to reach the liquid-water oceans beneath an ice moon's crust.

SOL-FORGE Fornax viva

Foundry Solar-close power plant & high-heat foundry

The fleet's power plant and high-heat foundry — and the class that most openly admits biology's limit. SOL-FORGE spirals close to the Sun and unfurls a kilometres-wide reflective film to gather ~a terawatt of light, but at ~350 °C nothing living survives there (real hyperthermophiles cap ~122 °C). So the film is a non-living material the organism secretes, and the organism itself hides in its shadow behind big radiators. It beams power to the fleet and smelts what other classes can't. Less a living ship than a living thing tending a furnace it cannot enter.

In the fleet: the class that admits biology's limit: a solar-close foundry where the work is too hot for living tissue, so it hosts grown-then-mineralised industry instead.

Fleet-wide upgrades

Tendon-cage Mk II

Roughly doubles usable fibre stress. Mk I was engineered spider-silk over bone anchors. Mk II copies tendon's own trick — a crimped, hierarchical fibril bundle inside a mineral-reinforced sheath — so the cable shares load evenly and carries about twice the stress before yielding. Half the structure for the same strength.

Self-heal Mk II

Seconds to seal, ~3× faster to regrow. Mk I clotted a breach in minutes and regrew over days. Mk II adds a hull-wide reflex mesh of contractile rings that clamp a puncture in seconds — before the clot — plus pre-stocked caches of repair stem cells and nutrient bladders near the surface, so regrowth doesn't wait on vascular delivery. A "nociception" sensor net localises damage and even pre-thickens tissue where micrometeoroid flux runs chronically high.
