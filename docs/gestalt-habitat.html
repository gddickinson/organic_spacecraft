PLATE 0

Cutaway · the interior world

¾ CUTAWAY · NOT TO SCALE

A section of the drum removed. You live on the inside of the spinning cylinder: the ground curves up on both sides and closes overhead into a sky of far-side landscape. There are no windows — a luminous sun-cord runs the axis, dimming for night. Below the soil lies the layered living rim (Plate IV).

PLATE I

General arrangement · external

SCALE 1 : 66,700 (1 px ≈ 16.7 m)
SIX GROWN SEGMENTS @ ~1.7 km

The whole organism from outside: a segmented drum, grown somite by somite like a stalk, wrapped in a regolith shield. De-spun docking hubs cap each axis; the aft cap is moored to the asteroid it is eating. At this scale the starship GESTALT is a speck and a human is invisible.

PLATE II

Longitudinal section · biomes & axis

SECTION ON AXIS · SCALE 1 : 66,700

The drum opened along its axis. Land lines the whole inner surface — drawn here as the near and far shores of one ring world — divided into six climate biomes. Gravity fades from 1 g at the rim to 0 g on the axis, so the endcaps are terraced farms and the sun-cord and docks float weightless at the centre.

PLATE III

Transverse section · the ring world

END VIEW · SCALE 1 : 20,000 (1 px ≈ 10 m)

The defining diagram: a slice across the drum. Everyone lives on the inner rim, held down by spin. Read the radial ladder outward — 0 g on the axis but, because the 2.5 km radius is far shorter than the air's ~8.4 km scale height, still a breathable ~45 kPa there — rising only gently to 52 kPa and 1 g at the rim. The habitable world is the green ring at the edge, but the whole volume holds air.

PLATE IV

Rim wall · the ground you stand on

DETAIL 1 · SCALE 1 : 130 · DEEP SPACE (LEFT) → SOIL (RIGHT)

A radial core through the ~33 m rim. Most of the depth is the tendon cage — a woven skeleton carrying 230 MN per metre of hoop tension: 130 from air pressure (P·R) plus ~100 from the spin-weight of the soil, water and structures pressing outward at 1 g. That needs ~2.3 m of silk-bone fibre at a 100 MPa working stress[8][9][10] — halved to ~1.15 m by the Mk II upgrade. Beneath it runs a habitable service deck of transit, water mains and vascular trunks. Note what the stack does not need: a dedicated radiation shield — the ~300 g/cm² regolith over-blanket is the real shield[6][7], and the ~140 g/cm² radial column of overhead air adds to it (~440 g/cm² together, comparable to several metres of water), so the habitat is self-shielded by the world around and inside it.

PLATE V

Detail studies · systems that make a world

DETAILS 2–6 · VARIOUS SCALES

How the habitat lights itself, carries its own weight, grows new land, docks ships, and what a district actually looks like from the ground.

Axial sun-cordDET. 2

A weightless luminous tube on the axis. Lighting ~100 km² of interior to a moderate daylight of ~150 W/m²[2] takes about 15 GW, fed mainly by concentrated starlight piped from a collector at the sunward endcap — ~3.7 km across at 1 AU, and scaling to ~10 km out in the asteroid belt where sunlight is ~7× weaker. That 150 W/m² closes the oxygen loop with room to spare: the biomes need only ~8 g O₂/m²·day, roughly a 2% photosynthetic efficiency. Bioluminescent organs add only a dim safety glow, since biology tops out under 1 W/m². A muscular sheath dims the whole cord to make day and night.

Tendon-cage nodeDET. 3

Engineered spider-silk protein spun over a mineralized bone anchor — a grown ligament-to-bone joint. The helical cage of these carries the ring's entire outward load in pure tension.

Segment growth collarDET. 4

Segments meet at living collars of dividing cells. To house more people the habitat grows another somite — a new biome — from the collar, so the world lengthens over its lifetime.

De-spun docking hubDET. 5

Ships can't dock to a spinning rim, so the axis hub runs on a living bearing that cancels the spin — weightless and stationary. Passengers ride "down" the endcap into gravity.

District — from the groundDET. 6

At ground level it reads as ordinary countryside — until you notice the horizon rising instead of falling, farms and lakes hanging in the sky, and a line of light where a sun should be.

PLATE VI

Unrolled biome map · the plan view

INNER SURFACE FLATTENED · 15.7 × 10 km
SCALE 1 : 20,000

The cylinder's inner surface peeled off and laid flat: 15.7 km of circumference (the left edge wraps around to the right) by 10 km end to end. Six climate biomes ring the drum in sequence; a transit trunk runs the length between the de-spun hubs, and water gathers at the rim into ring-lakes and rivers. Each biome rings the full 15.7 km circumference over ~1.7 km of length — about 26 km² apiece — and the Urban stripe holds roughly 60% of the million people at a dense ~36,000/km².

Drawing conventions

Green = living / photosynthetic tissue · cyan = engineered systems & data · amber = structure, fluid & shielding. Chain-dashed lines are centrelines; dashed lines are hidden edges. Each plate carries a title block (drawing no. · scale · revision); all sheets are CONCEPT — not for construction.

Why this shape

A million people need spin gravity over a huge floor, so the grown egg becomes a grown drum. Living on the inside of a spinning cylinder gives ~1 g at the rim; the far side of the land becomes the sky. Same organism, new body plan.

Scale caveat

Orthographic plates are dimensioned; in the section views the rim stack is drawn far thicker than 1:scale (it is only ~33 m on a 5,000 m radius — a hair on the drawing). Plate 0 and the district study are perspective illustrations.

The hard part

At this scale biology alone can't hold the hoop load (~230 MN/m); the tendon cage likely needs engineered high-tensile cable, possibly reinforced. Gestation runs to decades and gigatonnes of in-situ material. Conceptual, not a build spec.

Life at 0.6 rpm

The spin is gentle enough to live in: Coriolis force on a walking person is only ~1.8% of gravity, and a ball dropped from 2 m lands ~5 cm spinward. And because the drum's 2.5 km radius is far shorter than the air's ~8.4 km scale height, the atmosphere barely thins with altitude — 52 kPa at the rim, still ~45 kPa and breathable on the 0-g axis. Spinning the ~2.5 Gt drum up to 0.6 rpm costs a one-time ~3×10¹⁶ J (~9 TWh, ~300 MW averaged over a 3-year spin-up) — then it simply keeps turning.

The air is a century-scale buffer

A million people breathe ~840 t of O₂ and exhale ~1,000 t of CO₂ a day[1]. Against that, the ~113 Mt atmosphere is still enormous: at its 34% O₂ it holds ~42 Mt of O₂ — about 140 years' supply if photosynthesis stopped entirely — and CO₂ would take ~3 years to build to a hazardous 1%. The habitat's air is a giant flywheel; short outages of the living wall are non-events, not emergencies.

The tight loop is food, not air

Oxygen is easy — ~56 km² of managed vegetation covers the crew's demand[3], well within the 100 km² of landscape. Food is the real constraint: feeding a million at open-field intensity would need ~150 km² (more surface than exists), so the habitat leans on multi-tier endcap farms (~15–30 km²). And full closure at this scale is unproven — BIOS-3 ran 2–3 crew for months at partial closure; Biosphere-2's oxygen crashed from 21% to 14% in two years.[4][5] Buffered, but not solved.

THE PUBLISHED BASIS

ARCA's life-support, shielding and light budgets rest on real, published standards and results; the spin-gravity geometry is textbook mechanics (ω²R). Each cited claim links here. Full closure at population scale is unproven — that gap is stated in the text, not cited away.
