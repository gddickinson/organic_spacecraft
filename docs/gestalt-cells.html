Cell Atlas · Cytology & Histology

The Cells That Are the Ship

A grown vessel is not filled with life — it is built out of it. This is the catalogue of every cell type the body needs: what each one is, how it lives and survives, how they coordinate into one organism, how they are distributed and fed, and how they would be engineered, grown and tested.

The other GESTALT documents describe organs — a hull, a lung, a skeleton, a nursery. This one goes one level down, to the cells those organs are made of. A single NAVIS hull is on the order of 1019 cells; the whole crewed vessel is a small planet's worth of them. They come in about 42 distinct types across eight functional classes, and — like every animal, plant and fungus — all of them descend by division and differentiation from the handful of cells packed into one seed. What follows is their census: role, survival, metabolism, life-cycle, coordination, and distribution, then how each would be engineered, grown and tested. Where a cell type maps to a real, transformable organism or a demonstrated technique it is marked green; where the integration is a genuine bet it is marked gap. This document assumes the chassis organisms and gene circuits set out in the Compendium.

01 The plan of the body

One seed, many cells

Biology already solves the hard version of this problem: a human is ~37 trillion cells of ~200 types, all clonal descendants of one zygote, arranged into organs by a growth program. GESTALT borrows that architecture wholesale. The seed carries a small population of totipotent founder cells and a genome encoding the whole body plan; gestation is a controlled cascade of division and differentiation that reads position and stress and turns founders into the right cell, in the right place, in the right number.

The division of labour is the same one the Compendium sets out at the organism level — a designed consortium of a structural fungus, photosynthetic microbes, mineralising cells and a few specialists — but here each is resolved into the specific differentiated cell types it produces. The consortium is real; the differentiation program that places 1019 cells into a shaped, self-maintaining body is the single largest bet in the whole enterprise (§11).

From one founder to forty-two types. Every cell in the vessel is a clonal descendant of the seed's totipotent founders; differentiation is directed by position, stress and morphogen gradients during gestation (§10). Colour marks the broad job: green = living structure & metabolism, cyan = transport, sensing & coordination, amber = mineral, defence & resource work.

The census — a NAVIS hull

The 120 m × 50 m hull is a ~5.5 m-thick living shell of ~15,800 m² area and ~87,000 m³ volume. At tissue densities of ~1012/m³ (sparse mycelial matrix) to ~1014/m³ (denser cellular tissue), that is ~5×1018 cells in the hull alone — before the decks, vasculature and interior. The design target is not to specify each one, but to specify ~42 types and the rules that place and renew them.

02 Structural cells

Skin, wood, bone and the seal

The bulk of the vessel by mass is structure, and structure is the province of cells that build, then often become, their own material — like the cells that make wood, bone, bark and silk on Earth. They read mechanical stress and lay material along the load, so the hull is not merely strong but self-optimising.[1]

03 Metabolic & light cells

The air plant, and the cells that feed everyone else

Only one class of cell brings new energy and carbon into the body: the phototrophs of the photosynthetic intima. Everything else — every structural, vascular, sensing and defence cell — is a heterotroph living on the sugar and oxygen they export. The whole trophic web (§08) hangs from this layer, which is why light is the fleet's hard range limit.

04 Vascular & transport cells

The plumbing that keeps metres of tissue alive

Metres of living tissue cannot survive by diffusion — the Krogh limit is ~200 µm. So the body is threaded by a branching vascular tree obeying Murray's law, with capillaries ~400 µm apart, so no cell is more than ~100–200 µm from a supply channel.[10] Building and running that network is a job for its own cell types.

05 Sensing, signalling & coordination

The nervous system of a grown ship

A body of 1019 cells needs to act as one: aim its windows at the star, seal a breach in seconds, throttle metabolism to demand, and — over years — keep its own genome from drifting. GESTALT has no brain; coordination is distributed across three overlapping systems working on three timescales.

Growth · morphogens

During gestation, organizer cells emit diffusing morphogens; reaction–diffusion (Turing) gradients and contact signalling (synNotch) tell each cell what to become and where — the body plan as chemistry.[11]

Runtime · the signalling net

A slow electro-chemical network — modelled on plant electrical signalling and fungal mycelial conduction[12] — carries reflexes: breach → seal, low light → phototropism, strain → remodel. A distributed reflex net, not a mind.

Deep time · CHORUS

A protected germline archive keeps a master copy of the genome and periodically re-syncs somatic lineages, so mutation and drift do not accumulate over a vessel's long life (the sustainability backstop).

06 Defence & immune cells

Policing 1019 cells

Growing a whale-and-beyond mass of dividing cells makes cancer and infection existential, not incidental. The defence is the internal twin of the fleet's biocontainment problem, and it is enforced by dedicated cells plus circuits every cell carries (the seven-layer control described in the Compendium).

08 The internal ecosystem

A managed microbiome, not just a machine

The tissue cells above are only half the census. The body also carries a microbiome of free-living guilds that close the element loops — exactly the microbes an Earth soil or a closed life-support system runs on. They are not decoration: without them the nitrogen and carbon cycles do not close, and the body starves on its own waste.[17]

The internal trophic web. Only the intima (green) brings in new energy; everything else eats its output or recycles waste. Two inputs cross the boundary — light and rock/ice — and the microbial guilds close the nitrogen and carbon loops so only leakage needs topping up.

The honest hard part

An ecosystem of dozens of engineered types coexisting stably for decades, without one lineage outcompeting, mutating or crashing the others, is unproven. Biosphere-2's oxygen crashed in two years; BIOS-3 and MELiSSA held closure only for months at small scale.[17] Long-term closed-ecology stability is the goal of Earth-Program WP2, not a solved problem. gap

09 Life-cycle, turnover & distribution

How cells are placed, renewed and retired

Every cell type above shares a common life-cycle grammar borrowed from real tissue: it is born from a progenitor, differentiates into role, works, and is eventually renewed or retired by programmed death. Three rules keep 1019 cells honest.

Distribution — the Krogh rule

No cell may sit >~200 µm from a vascular channel, so tissue is laid down with its plumbing. Cell density follows the layer: sparse in the mycelial matrix, dense at the intima and osteoid.

Turnover — renewal pools

Each tissue keeps a small progenitor pool for steady replacement; the epidermis renews fast (it is being ablated), the osteocytes and CHORUS core almost never divide.

Retirement — the division cap

Somatic lineages carry a Hayflick/telomere cap and armed apoptosis, so no line divides forever — the same brake that stops cancer also bounds senescence, refreshed only by CHORUS re-sync.

Where the cells live. The six-layer hull (compare the Dossier anatomy) mapped to its resident cell types, with the vascular tree threading every layer so no cell is beyond the ~200 µm diffusion limit. Regeneration re-runs this same program locally at a wound.

Repair is not a separate system — it is the growth program, aimed at a hole. Damage exposes matrix and drops pressure; wound-response cells seal it; a blastema of reactivated stem cells re-forms, organizer cells re-establish the local morphogen field, and the tissue regrows layer by layer, stopping when size-sensing and the division cap say the shape is restored.

10 Engineering, growing & testing

From a strain in a flask to a shaped body

Each cell type is reached the same way: start from a real, transformable chassis; add characterised gene circuits for its job; wire in the containment and differentiation controls; then prove it at increasing scale. The chassis and parts are the Compendium's subject; here is the cell-level pipeline.

11 What is real, and what is a bet

Nearly every individual cell type here maps to a real organism or a demonstrated technique — mycelium materials, sucrose-exporting cyanobacteria, MICP mineralisation, biomining acidophiles, radiotrophic melanin, CAR recognition, synNotch patterning, crystallin optics. That is the green in the tables. The bets are in the integration, and they are large:

Bet 1 · directed morphogenesis

Turning a seed into a specified, metre-scale shape — placing 1019 cells by morphogen — does not exist yet. It is the single biggest gate, and the Earth Program tests it first. gap

Bet 2 · consortium stability

Dozens of engineered types coexisting for decades without one mutating, drifting or crashing the ecosystem is unproven at any scale. gap

Bet 3 · the coordination net

A body-wide signalling system that reliably carries reflexes across kilometres of tissue is speculative — plant/fungal signalling is real but slow and simple. gap

The honest summary: the parts bin is real; assembling it into one coordinated, self-maintaining, cancer-proof body is the enterprise. This document is a specification of the parts and the rules — not a claim that the body has been built. It hasn't.
