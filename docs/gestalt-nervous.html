Nervous System · Cognition, Control & Comms

The Wet Brain and the Dry

A grown ship must sense, decide, act, and talk — across kilometres, in real time, with people and machines. Biology is brilliant at sensing and staying alive but slow at arithmetic; silicon is the reverse. So a GESTALT vessel is, honestly, a cyborg: a grown nervous system fused to a fabricated computer.

The other documents grow a body. This one asks how that body knows anything and acts on it. The honest answer refuses a single clean substrate: growing a fast, precise, radio-fluent flight computer out of cells is not on the cards, and running a whole living ship on transistors throws away everything biology is good at. GESTALT therefore splits the mind by what each material does best — a grown nervous system for sensing, homeostasis, reflex and learning, and a fabricated silicon core for fast deterministic control, communications and the human digital world — joined by a real, if immature, bio-electronic interface. Established science is marked green; the integration is marked gap.

01 The architecture

A layered, hybrid mind — not a brain in a jar

There is no central brain. Coordination is spread across four layers, each on the substrate and timescale that suits its job — fast local reflexes at the bottom, slow global homeostasis and learning in the middle, and a dry silicon core at the top for everything biology is bad at. Each layer is as autonomous as it can be, so a severed or slow link never leaves the ship helpless.

The control stack. Reflexes and homeostasis stay wet (grown, parallel, self-repairing); fast deterministic compute, navigation and radio stay dry (fabricated silicon); the two meet at a bio-electronic interface. The design rule: push every task to the layer that is good at it, and let each layer run on its own if the others fall silent.

02 Senses

What the ship can feel

Sensing is the one job biology already does superbly — evolution has built eyes, ears, noses and magnetic compasses far beyond our sensors. A grown vessel can grow real sense organs, distributed as a skin of receptors and concentrated into imaging "eyes" where it needs to look.

03 Signalling

How a signal travels — and why speed is the catch

Biology already signals electrically — this is not a metaphor. Plants fire action potentials; fungi conduct electrical spikes along their mycelium; bacterial biofilms coordinate with travelling waves of potassium ion-channel signalling; and "cable bacteria" conduct electrons along centimetre-long conductive fibres.[4][5] A grown ship carries three signalling systems layered by speed:

Fast · bioelectric

Action-potential-analogues along grown nerve-tracts and the mycelial net carry reflexes: breach → seal, contact → grip. Milliseconds to seconds, but local.

Slow · chemical & hormonal

Broadcast molecules through the vasculature set body-wide state — grow here, throttle there. Minutes to hours; global.

The catch · latency

Nerve conduction tops out around tens of m/s; plant/fungal signalling is far slower. Across a 10 km ARCA that is seconds to minutes end-to-end — far too slow for fast global control.

This latency is the whole reason the architecture is layered and hybrid: anything needing millisecond, ship-wide coordination — attitude, engine timing — cannot wait for a wet signal to cross kilometres, so it lives on the fast silicon bus (§05) or is handled by local autonomy. The wet nervous system governs what is slow by nature: growth, healing, metabolism, mood.

04 Growing computation

Can it grow a brain? Yes — a certain kind

Grown computation is real, and it is genuinely good at some things and hopeless at others. GESTALT uses four kinds, each already demonstrated in the lab:

Kind of organic computerWhat it isReal basis
Neural tissueGrown neurons wire into trainable networks — living neural nets that learn. Cultured cortical neurons on an electrode array learned to play Pong.[6]DishBrain; brain organoids
Genetic logicCells run Boolean logic and memory in gene circuits — AND/OR gates, toggle switches, counters. Slow, but astronomically parallel across 10¹⁹ cells.Synthetic gene circuits[7]
Reservoir / physicalThe vascular or mycelial network itself is a dynamical system whose response can be read out as computation ("reservoir computing"; fungal computing).[8]Reservoir computing; fungal computing
Bioelectric / morphogeneticCells use bioelectric networks to store and compute anatomical goals — the substrate of growth & repair decisions.[9]Levin bioelectric pattern control
What the wet brain is great at

Massively parallel pattern recognition, adaptive & homeostatic control, learning, self-repair, radiation tolerance (it heals), and extreme energy efficiency (a brain runs on ~20 W).

What it is bad at

Speed, precision, deterministic timing, high-bandwidth I/O, and exact arithmetic. You would not fly a docking burn or run a radio link on it. gap

So the wet brain is the ship's intuition and physiology — the thing that keeps it alive, recognises a face or a fault, and learns a crew's habits — not its flight computer.

05 Silicon & the interface

The dry brain, and how to wire it to the wet

Some jobs simply demand silicon: a docking burn timed to the millisecond, navigation and clocks, cryptography, a megabit radio link, and — crucially — talking to the digital systems humans and other craft already use. Biology cannot do these well, and it cannot grow a modern transistor: high-performance logic needs a fab. (Cells can biomineralise semiconductors and template quantum dots and magnetite,[10] but not a CPU.) So the vessel carries a fabricated silicon / neuromorphic core — an inorganic payload planted with the seed, like a stone in a fruit — and grows its tissue around and into it.

The hard, interesting part is the bio-electronic interface: biology speaks in ions and molecules, electronics in electrons and voltages. Transducing between them is a real and active field.

Ions ↔ electrons. Five real transduction routes bridge the wet and dry brains: organic electrochemical transistors & conducting polymers, microelectrode arrays, electrogenetics, opto-genetic/opto-electronic light links, and genetically-encoded conductive nanowires. Each works today at electrode or organoid scale; running them at spacecraft bandwidth & reliability is the frontier.

The honest shape

The ship grows the sensors, the reflexes, the homeostat and the adaptive brain; it carries and integrates the fast compute and the radios; and the membrane between them — the bio-electronic interface — is exactly where today's science is thin. GESTALT is a cyborg by necessity, not for show.

06 Communication

Talking — to the crew and to other craft

Communication splits the same way as everything else: the rich, ambient, human channels can be grown; the fast, long-range, machine channels are electronic.

So a GESTALT vessel can be talked to like a person and queried like a computer: glowing a warning to the crew in the same second it transmits a coded telemetry burst to the fleet.

07 A worked loop

Coming in to dock — who does what

One manoeuvre shows the division of labour end to end: the wet side senses and holds the ship alive, the dry side computes the precise trajectory, and the wet side actuates.

The sense → compute → act loop of a docking. Wet senses and reflexes bracket a dry core that does the fast, precise trajectory maths; the interface carries commands each way. Nothing here is beyond a single substrate's competence — which is exactly why both are present.

eyes + magneto + proprioception → interface → silicon core: trajectory & timing → interface → bioelectric reflex: fire muscles & docking sphincters → homeostat holds pressure → loop at kHz

08 What is real, and what is a bet

Every component here exists: opsin eyes and mechano/chemo/magneto receptors; bioelectric signalling in plants, fungi and bacteria; neural tissue that learns; gene-circuit logic; OECTs, MEAs, electrogenetics, optogenetics and microbial nanowires; biomineralised semiconductors and magnetosome antennas. That is the green. The bets are in scale, speed and reliability:

Bet 1 · a reliable wet brain

Scaling grown neural tissue into a large, stable, trainable organ — and keeping it trained and healthy for decades — is far beyond a Pong-playing dish. gap

Bet 2 · the interface at rate

The bio-electronic interface works at electrode scale; running it at spacecraft bandwidth and reliability, radiation-hardened, is unproven. gap

Bet 3 · latency & autonomy

A km-scale body is latency-limited; making local autonomy add up to coherent whole-ship behaviour is a hard control problem. gap

The honest thesis: a GESTALT vessel thinks with two brains. Grow the one that senses, heals and adapts; fabricate and carry the one that computes and talks; and treat the interface between them as the real frontier — the same reliability wall, in the end, as the containment and coordination problems the rest of the program keeps running into. It is tested on the ground in the same Earth Program.
