Grown Vehicle Program · Fleet Registry & Development Log ROUND 23 · COMPLETE

The GESTALT Fleet

One biology, many bodies. Every vessel here is grown, not built — from a seed, on the rock it parked beside — sharing a common living platform but specialised into a different size and purpose. This registry grows by rounds; each entry carries a detailed protocol for how that design is actually gestated.

◆ The shared platform

What every hull inherits

All classes are the same organism dialect — a designed symbiosis grown from a dormant seed. They differ in which organs they emphasise, not in their underlying biology.

Chassis
Synthetic lichen: engineered fungus + cyanobacteria + calcifying bacteria, grown as one composite.
Structure
Mycelium scaffold + osteoid (collagen–hydroxyapatite) bone, tuned by load like a skeleton.
Air & food
Photosynthetic intima and biosphere; a materially-closed waste loop.
Skin
Melanized rind + water/regolith shielding; a sacrificial, self-regrowing outer layer.
Repair
Clot-then-regrow self-healing; sphincter bulkheads seal and regrow lost compartments.
Seed
Dormant, radiation-hard husk of stem cells + error-corrected genome + starter culture.

The growth rates are grounded, not asserted — every class's pace is set by mining and deposition, and ultimately by its scarcest nutrient (the Compendium works through the phosphorus bottleneck).

NAVIS · deposition
~13 t/day average to grow a 24,000 t starship in ~5 yr; exponential while small, then capped by the mining/deposition rate so it plateaus rather than spiking (growth curve).
GRAVID · throughput
each cradle's placenta pumps ~26 t/day of feedstock; ~5 finished ships a year across a dozen cradles.
RADIX · biomining
Acidithiobacillus oxidises Fe/S to bioleach metals at tonnes/day — and spawns the seeds of every other class.
POMONA · yield
~18 km² of photosynthetic frond feeds ~100,000; just ~2.8 km² of algae covers their oxygen.

◆ How the fleet feeds itself

The supply web

Thirteen classes, one metabolism. Harvesters gather raw matter, the freighter moves it, the nursery grows it into ships, and the services keep them fed, healed, watched and backed up — while every class, including the harvesters and the nursery itself, is grown from a seed. The loop closes on itself.

◆ The bootstrap

From one seed to a civilisation

The whole self-reproducing fleet unfolds from a single ~120 kg launched seed. A miner germinates first and spawns more seeds; those grow a nursery; the nursery grows the ships and habitats; and by roughly year fifty the ecosystem is self-sustaining — because every stage grows the next, capability compounds.

◆ Platform upgrades

Development · the Mk II platform

Improvements to the shared biology, not new vessels. Because every class expresses the same genome, an upgrade here propagates to all of them — and grown hulls adopt it by remodelling over time, the way bone rebuilds.

Tendon-cage Mk II

Mk I was engineered spider-silk over bone anchors. Mk II copies tendon's own trick — a crimped, hierarchical fibril bundle inside a mineral-reinforced sheath — so the cable shares load evenly and carries about twice the stress before yielding. Half the structure for the same strength.
Rollout

CHALLENGEGrowing a defect-free hierarchical weave at kilometre scale; proving the stress before trusting the thinner cage.

The bricks are real — most components sit at TRL 3–5, and several have flown. The mortar is the gap: coordinating them into one self-growing organism at metre-to-kilometre scale — vasculature and morphogenesis — sits at TRL ~2 and is the program's long pole. That single line is why GESTALT is a vision built on real science, not a build spec.

The Unlocked by column maps each capability onto the ground program that would mature it (see the Earth Program). The pattern is the whole story: nine of twelve rows mature in Phase 0–1 — the cheap, mostly-fundable early science — while the only two rows still at TRL 2, vasculature and morphogenesis (⚑), are exactly what the Phase-2 go/no-go gate exists to test. The fleet's weakest bars and the program's make-or-break moment are the same place.

◆ A cross-cutting question

Every class runs on photosynthesis, so every class has to get daylight-level light to living tissue that is usually behind heavy shielding. There is no single answer — the delivery method is set by the vessel's size, spin and distance from the Sun. Four strategies cover the fleet.

None — go darkBeyond the inner system, sunlight is too weak; photosynthesis gives way to stored or other energyDeep-space / outer-system missionsFlux drops below the ~150 W/m² daylight floor past ~3 AU (flux ∝ 1/r²)

The two curves are the whole argument. Sunlight (cyan) falls as 1/r² and crosses the ~150 W/m² daylight floor at ~3 AU — so out to roughly the asteroid belt a vessel can still photosynthesise on collected light, but the collector needed to hold a fixed 15 GW (amber) grows in step with distance: 3.7 km at 1 AU, ~10 km in the belt, ~35 km at Saturn. Past the belt the collector becomes impractical and life support must switch energy sources. It is the same principle end to end: bring light through the shield when the craft is small, around it when the craft is big or buried — and accept that deep space is dark.

◆ The hardest part

Governance & containment

A self-reproducing living technology is the most dangerous idea in this entire document. If GESTALT were ever more than a concept, these constraints would have to come first — not bolted on afterward, but as the precondition for growing the very first seed. Several exist at lab scale today (see the readiness scorecard); governing them across a fleet spread over light-hours does not.

Risk · runaway growth

No seed germinates unbidden

Every seed carries a cryptographic lock; it will not germinate, and GRAVID will not gestate it, without an external, signed authorization. The fleet cannot decide to reproduce on its own — each new vessel is a licensed act, so exponential growth stays a choice, not a default.

Residual: forging or coercing the licensing authority; a lock is only as sound as its keys.
Risk · lineage runaway

Hayflick limits, at every scale

Somatic tissue carries division caps like ordinary cells, and every lineage carries a generation counter. A line that outlives its licence, or drifts from spec, senesces rather than spreading — the same brake that stops one organism becoming a tumour, applied to the fleet becoming a plague.

Residual: a mutation that disables the counter — which is why the genome policing below matters.
Risk · escape / contamination

Built to depend on us, unable to mix

The organisms are engineered to require a synthetic nutrient found nowhere in nature (real: recoded auxotrophs) and to use an altered genetic code, so they cannot survive unsupervised in a wild biosphere nor exchange genes with natural life. A NEREUS diver loose in an alien ocean would starve, not colonise.

Residual: evolution routes around containment given enough generations — hence the hard limits.
Risk · drift / cancer

A master genome that never forgets

The canonical genome is stored with error correction in CHORUS and re-synchronised into the germline periodically; somatic drift and cancerous growth are policed by enforced apoptosis. The organisms are held to their design across generations the way a text is held by a checksum.

Residual: who is authorised to change the canon — a governance question, not a technical one.
Risk · tyranny or schism

No single sovereign, no fragmentation

Decisions about what the fleet grows ride on CHORUS consensus across worlds, bounded by a charter — avoiding both a single authority that could weaponise the reproduction licence and a fragmentation into rival, diverging fleets. The hardest balance in the whole design, and the least solved.

Residual: this is a political problem across light-hours; no mechanism guarantees it.
Risk · no off-switch

Neglect ends it gracefully

The fleet depends on a periodically-renewed external signal and its synthetic-nutrient supply; withdraw them and it senesces gently rather than dying dangerously or running wild. The default state of an un-tended fleet is dormancy, not catastrophe — the off-switch is doing nothing.

Residual: a fleet that engineered around its own dependence would have defeated the point.

Containment is not a feature of the GESTALT fleet. It is the price of admission — and the honest position is that you do not grow the first seed until it is solved. On the ground, that discipline is rehearsed as the leading work package and its go/no-go gates in the Earth Program.

◆ Development log

Rounds of innovation

The program iterates on a schedule. Each round adds new classes or develops existing ones; this log records what changed.

ROUND 23 · 2026-07-26 · the end

Coda — the program is complete, and the loop stops here

With the safety capstone in place, the backlog is empty of anything substantive, so this round gives the program a proper ending rather than inventing filler: a closing statement, formally marking it complete at eighteen classes across every environment, two platform upgrades, six program-level studies, and six cross-linked documents. The 5-minute innovation loop that built rounds 1–23 has done its job and is being stopped. The work stands on its own; nothing essential remains to add.

ROUND 22 · 2026-07-26 · the hardest part

Governance & containment — the constraints that come first

The one substantive question the program had left unanswered, and the scariest: a self-reproducing living technology is the most dangerous thing in this whole document. Added a governance section laying out the constraints that would have to exist before any of this is built — reproduction licences that gate every germination, hard generation limits, synthetic auxotrophy and xenobiology so the organisms can't survive or spread uncontrolled, a canonical error-correcting genome, distributed consensus with no single sovereign, and a reversible off-switch. Several exist at lab scale; governing them across light-hours does not. The honest position: containment is not an add-on, it's the precondition — you don't grow the first seed until it's solved.

ROUND 21 · 2026-07-26 · paying down debt

The scale chart, rebuilt

A housekeeping round. The "Fleet to scale" ruler had grown crowded and overlapping as classes accumulated over the rounds, so it's been rebuilt as a clean sorted chart — every class on one logarithmic size axis, smallest to largest, colour-coded by role (crewed, infrastructure, sensing). No new class; just making eighteen fit legibly on one line, from the 4 m SPORE to the 40 km LEVIATHAN. Sometimes development means tidying rather than adding.

ROUND 20 · 2026-07-26 · the furnace

SOL-FORGE — the class that admits biology's limit

Added SOL-FORGE, a solar-close power plant and high-heat foundry that unfurls a kilometres-wide reflective film near the Sun to gather ~a terawatt of light. It is the most honest class in the fleet about where the living premise breaks: nothing living survives solar-close heat (real hyperthermophiles cap ~122 °C), so the collector is a non-living material the organism secretes, and the organism hides in its shadow behind big radiators — a living thing tending a furnace it cannot enter. Twenty rounds in, this feels like the right note to rest on: even a fleet of living ships has an edge where biology hands off to plain physics.

ROUND 19 · 2026-07-26 · into the ocean

NEREUS — the fleet reaches the water under the ice

Added NEREUS, an ice-moon ocean diver: it lands on Europa or Enceladus, grows a hot melt-head that bores down through kilometres of ice while its tail refreezes the channel behind, then swims the ~150 MPa ocean as a pressure-equalized organism — no gas voids, like a Mariana-trench fish built deeper. It explores, samples vents, and searches for native life. Its defining problem is not engineering but ethics: putting a living, engineered organism into a possibly-inhabited alien ocean is the gravest act of planetary protection the whole program could commit — and the honest answer may be "don't." Next: optional polish, or a natural place to stop.

ROUND 18 · 2026-07-26 · the leverage

Seed mass vs grown mass — the whole thesis in one chart

A development round that visualises the idea the program has rested on since the first dossier: you launch almost nothing and grow almost everything. On a log scale, every class's seed (grams to hundreds of tonnes) sits four to nine orders of magnitude left of what it becomes. One of each: ~352 tonnes of seeds → ~14.5 gigatonnes of vessels. The extreme is ARCA — a 3-tonne seed becomes a 2.4-gigatonne world, a leverage of ~800,000,000 to one. This is why growing beats building for anything far from Earth: rockets move the seed, the destination provides the rest.

ROUND 17 · 2026-07-26 · the shield

TESTUDO — a guardian that only ever defends

Added TESTUDO, the fleet's tortoise: a heavily armoured escort that protects the vulnerable — a farm, a liner full of sleepers, a young thin-shelled habitat, or a crew riding out a solar storm — by interposing its regrowing carapace or opening a shelter cavity. Whipple layers and a deep water jacket give it ~1,000 g/cm² (~50× what a solar storm requires, ~150,000 t), and VESPER-derived senses let it spot debris and nudge the fleet clear. It is strictly defensive by design: it survives hits and dodges hazards — it carries no weapons and intercepts nothing. This is the fleet's answer to the one thing biology can't out-grow: a bad day in a debris field or a solar storm.

ROUND 16 · 2026-07-26 · the memory

CHORUS — backing up the culture, not the genome

Added CHORUS, the fleet's living memory: a distributed archive that keeps its shared knowledge, law, language and art coherent across light-hours and generations. It complements TARDIGRADE — that vault backs up DNA, CHORUS backs up meaning. It writes to grown DNA archives (real DNA data storage fits ~an exabyte in a few grams) and reconciles versions across nodes through VESPER's mesh. The striking part is the honest one: the storage is trivial — grams hold a civilisation's whole record — while the real challenge is social, keeping that record from fragmenting across the distances between worlds. This completes the fleet's backup pair: genome and culture. Next: an optional defensive TESTUDO guardian.

ROUND 15 · 2026-07-26 · the bootstrap

Mission timeline — one seed to a civilisation in ~50 years

A systems round: the program's mission timeline, showing how the whole self-reproducing fleet unfolds from a single ~120 kg launched seed. A RADIX miner germinates first and spawns more seeds; those grow a GRAVID nursery; the nursery grows the first ships, farms and habitats; and by ~year 50 the ecosystem is self-sustaining and self-reproducing — growing new fleets from local resources, needing nothing further from Earth. The point the diagram makes: capability compounds because every stage grows the next. That closes the program's arc — a natural place to pause, or to keep widening.

ROUND 14 · 2026-07-26 · the sleepers

AMBER — carry a person for 3 tonnes, not 2,400

Added AMBER, a passenger liner that makes the opposite bet from a habitat: instead of keeping people alive and awake behind gigatonnes of world, it puts ~50,000 of them to sleep in an amber-like resin — trehalose glass, tardigrade Dsup shielding their DNA — and carries each for ~3 t instead of ARCA's ~2,400, roughly 900× less. It needs no biosphere, only a small crew and a revival organ. Honesty demands the caveat: this is the fleet's deepest reach — mammals don't do anhydrobiosis, and reversible human stasis is far beyond today's science (see the readiness scorecard). Next: a fleet-ops / mission-timeline round.

ROUND 13 · 2026-07-26 · a reality check

Technology readiness — how real is any of this?

A development round that scores the honesty of the whole program: each shared-platform component rated against what today's biology can actually do. The encouraging half — mycelium composites, algal life support, self-healing bioconcrete, radiotrophic melanin, biomining — sits at TRL 3–5, and several have literally flown on the ISS. The sobering half — self-organising metre-scale vasculature and coordinated kilometre-scale morphogenesis — sits at TRL ~2 and is the program's long pole. GESTALT is a vision built on real bricks, not a build spec. Next: AMBER, a long-haul passenger stasis liner.

ROUND 12 · 2026-07-26 · the womb, drawn

GRAVID gets its own drawing set

The nursery — the class that grows all the others — now has a full drawing set: a section through a placental gestation cradle with a NAVIS starship 60% grown inside (fed ~26 t/day through an umbilical), a plan of cradles budding from the feedstock spine at every stage, and a five-step seed-to-ship sequence. It shows the reproduction loop up close — how a seed becomes a ship in ~2–3 years instead of five. Next: a development round scoring the fleet's readiness.

ROUND 11 · 2026-07-26 · systems

The supply web — thirteen classes, one metabolism

A systems round rather than a new class: a diagram of how the fleet feeds itself. Harvesters (RADIX, MEDUSA) gather raw matter, ATLAS moves it, GRAVID grows it into ships, and the services — POMONA feeds, CORAL heals, VESPER watches, TARDIGRADE backs up — keep the living vessels running. The closing point is the reproduction loop: GRAVID grows every class, including the harvesters and itself, so the whole program is one self-sustaining organism. Next: a fuller standalone drawing set for GRAVID, the nursery.

ROUND 10 · 2026-07-26 · eyes and ears

VESPER — the fleet grows a nervous system

Added VESPER, a distributed constellation of small sensor nodes that give the fleet sight, hearing and a voice. Each grows a magnetite-laced dish for radio (magnetotactic bacteria biomineralise magnetite for real), lens-and-retina 'eyes' for light, and a bioluminescent beacon to signal. Deployed in hundreds across millions of kilometres, they act as one ~microarcsecond interferometer and a mesh relay — comms, navigation, and an early-warning watch that feeds targets to RADIX and MEDUSA. Its hard problem is making biology do radio: growing conductive, figure-perfect antennas out of tissue. Next: a program supply-web diagram.

ROUND 9 · 2026-07-26 · logistics

ATLAS — the freight that closes the ecosystem

Added ATLAS, the bulk hauler and tanker: a grown keel strung with modular holds — pressurised for biomass and seeds, cryogenic for MEDUSA's ices, open for RADIX's ore pellets — each droppable and resealing. It carries ~0.5 Mt a trip and runs on its own cargo, electrolysing water for reaction mass. With ATLAS the supply chain is whole: harvesters gather, ATLAS moves, GRAVID grows, habitats consume. Next: VESPER, the fleet's eyes and ears.

ROUND 8 · 2026-07-26 · development, not expansion

Platform Mk II — the whole fleet gets lighter and tougher

A development round rather than a new class. Tendon-cage Mk II borrows tendon's own crimped, hierarchical fibril structure and a mineral-reinforced sheath to roughly double usable fibre stress — halving the load-bearing cross-section and cutting ~12% off the mass of every spin habitat (≈290 Mt on ARCA, multi-gigatonne on LEVIATHAN), or banking it as extra hoop margin. Self-heal Mk II adds a hull-wide reflex mesh that clamps a breach in seconds and pre-stocked stem-cell caches that regrow layers ~3× faster. Both propagate through the shared genome to every class; grown hulls adopt them by remodelling, like bone. Next: ATLAS, a bulk cargo hauler.

ROUND 7 · 2026-07-26 · the volatile-drinker

MEDUSA — where RADIX eats rock, this one drinks ice

Added MEDUSA, a jellyfish-like harvester that gathers the ices and organics — water, carbon, nitrogen — the farms and habitats live on, completing the fleet's supply pairing: RADIX for metals and minerals, MEDUSA for volatiles. Kilometre-long tendrils grab comets and icy plumes; the bell sublimates, filters and stores. A fitting name — the chassis's bioluminescent organs already descend from Aequorea, the jellyfish that gave science GFP. Next: a development round — tendon-cage and self-heal Mk II.

ROUND 6 · 2026-07-26 · the reef

CORAL — where the fleet studies and heals itself

Added CORAL, a mid-size research and medical station grown like living coral into a calcium-carbonate reef — the chassis's calcifying bacteria doing openly what they do inside every hull. A slow ring gives 0–0.3 g for variable-gravity labs, a 0-g hub for microgravity work, and sealed polyp pods for biocontainment and medicine. Its hard problem is the opposite of the others': not growing life, but containing it. Next: MEDUSA, a free-flying comet-harvester.

ROUND 5 · 2026-07-26 · deep time

TARDIGRADE — a library that outlives its makers

Added TARDIGRADE, a dormancy vault built for geological time rather than a mission. Named for the water-bear, it borrows real survival tricks — trehalose glassification, the tardigrade Dsup protein that shields DNA, Deinococcus-grade repair — to carry the biosphere's genome library and a store of embryos through up to a million years, waking only to repair itself or reseed a dead world. It is the smallest class yet the densest: ~50 m but ~40,000 t, almost all shield. Next: CORAL, an orbital research and medical station.

ROUND 4 · 2026-07-26 · onto the ground

LICHEN — the first class that doesn't fly

Added LICHEN, a surface habitat grown into Moon or Mars regolith. Borrowing a planet's gravity lets it drop ARCA's entire spin apparatus and become the cheapest class to grow — a pressure blister ballasted and shielded by a regolith over-blanket, spreading dome by dome like a lichen crust. It gets its own drawing set. Next: TARDIGRADE, the deep-time seed vault.

ROUND 3 · 2026-07-25 · the colony tier

LEVIATHAN — ten worlds that travel as one

Added LEVIATHAN, a 10-million-person interstellar ark built as a colonial super-organism: twelve grown drums budded from a common trunk, siphonophore-style, each a spinning ~0.9 g world. It is the first design gestated by GRAVID rather than from a wild seed, and the first whose defining problem is time — genetic, ecological and cultural drift across a centuries-long cruise. Next: down to planetary surfaces with LICHEN.

ROUND 2 · 2026-07-25 · the reproduction hub

GRAVID — how the fleet builds the fleet

Added GRAVID, a nursery-shipyard that gestates other vessels from seeds in placental cradles, fed by RADIX miners. It closes the reproduction loop: RADIX eats rock and spawns seeds, GRAVID grows them into ships, and vessels that must be made faster no longer wait years in the wild. Next: scaling up to the generation-ark tier.

ROUND 1 · 2026-07-25 · founding registry

Platform consolidated; the size ladder opened at both ends

Formalised the shared living platform and admitted three new classes spanning the extremes the two flagships left open: SPORE (a 4 m lifeboat, gestated in weeks), RADIX (a crewless miner that also grows other ships' seeds), and POMONA (a near-massless agriculture station). One biology, many bodies

The point of the platform is reuse: the same seed and consortium make a lifeboat or a world. A design is mostly a recipe for which organs to grow large.

How to read a protocol

Seed = what launches. Feedstock = what it eats on arrival. Gestation = the morphogenesis sequence and time. Challenge = the class's defining unsolved bio-engineering problem.

Status

Conceptual throughout. Component biotech is real and mostly demonstrated; coordinated organisms at these scales are far beyond current synthetic biology. Not a build spec.

✦ Coda

The program is complete

It began as a single question — could you grow a living starship? — and became a whole ecosystem: eighteen classes of grown organism spanning deep space, orbit, planetary surfaces, the oceans beneath alien ice, and the fire near a star; able to mine, move, gestate, feed, heal, sense, remember, defend and reproduce — all from seeds you could carry in your hands.

Two things kept it honest. Every figure was checked against real science, and most of the biological bricks — mycelium, algae, self-healing concrete, radiotrophic melanin, tardigrade dormancy, DNA storage — genuinely exist, while the mortar that would bind them into one coordinated organism does not. And it ends not on triumph but on a constraint: a self-reproducing living technology is only ever as good as the containment that bounds it.
