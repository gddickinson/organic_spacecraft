PLATE I

Section through a dome

VERTICAL SECTION · ~1 : 3,300
MARS SURFACE SHOWN

Unlike every free-flying class, LICHEN borrows a planet's gravity, so there is no spin — just a low pressure blister rooted into the ground. The membrane barely feels the 52 kPa inside[4]; what it needs is a thick regolith over-blanket to shield it[6][7], ballast it against uplift, and roots that anchor to bedrock and tap subsurface ice — the in-situ resource use whose ground rehearsal is the Earth Program's WP5 (biomining, volatiles & the seed).

PLATE II

Colony plan · a spreading crust

PLAN VIEW · GROWTH MARGIN AT RIGHT

One dome is a village; the settlement is a colony. New domes bud from the rim and fuse to their neighbours through pressurised tunnels, so the town spreads across the regolith like a lichen thallus creeping over bare rock — older and larger toward the founder, youngest at the advancing margin.

PLATE III

Growth sequence · seed to settlement

FIVE STAGES · ~2–4 YR PER DOME

A one-tonne seed lands, roots, inflates, buries itself, greens, and buds a neighbour. With a whole planet's regolith and ice underfoot, it is the fastest and cheapest class to grow — the challenge is chemistry, not scale: Martian regolith must be stripped of its perchlorates before anything can live in it.

Why no spin

On a planet you already have gravity, so LICHEN drops the whole rotating apparatus of ARCA. It becomes the simplest, cheapest class — a pressure blister on the ground — and the only one that colonises a surface rather than flying free.

The real problem: chemistry

Martian regolith holds ~0.5–1% perchlorate[2][3] (ClO₄⁻), toxic to thyroids and most plants. Perchlorate-reducing bacteria (real: Dechloromonas, Azospira)[1] strip it stepwise — ClO₄⁻ → ClO₃⁻ → ClO₂⁻ → Cl⁻ + O₂ — and the final chlorite-dismutase step releases oxygen: detoxing the top ~1 m of one dome's floor liberates ~1,400 t of O₂, roughly 4,600 person-years' worth[8], so the poison doubles as a startup air supply. Bonding a living membrane to raw rock[5], and anchoring against uplift, are the other hard parts: the dome lifts with ~10 GN of pressure force (52 kPa over its ~0.2 km² footprint), held down by an over-blanket sized so its own weight balances that pressure — ρgh = 52 kPa gives ~9 m of regolith on Mars, ~21 m on the lower-gravity Moon.

THE PUBLISHED BASIS

LICHEN's headline trick — turning toxic Martian regolith into breathable oxygen — and its structural and shielding numbers rest on real, published work. Each cited claim links here.

  1. Coates, J. D. & Achenbach, L. A. (2004). Microbial perchlorate reduction: rocket-fuelled metabolism. Nat. Rev. Microbiol. 2, 569–580. — Dechloromonas/Azospira reduce ClO₄⁻ → Cl⁻, with chlorite dismutase releasing O₂.
  2. Hecht, M. H. et al. (2009). Detection of perchlorate and the soluble chemistry of martian soil at the Phoenix lander site. Science 325, 64–67. — Mars regolith ~0.4–0.6 wt% perchlorate.
  3. Davila, A. F., Willson, D., Coates, J. D. & McKay, C. P. (2013). Perchlorate on Mars: a chemical hazard and a resource for humans. Int. J. Astrobiol. 12, 321–325. — the detox-as-oxygen-supply premise.
  4. Gosline, J. M. et al. (1999). The mechanical design of spider silks. J. Exp. Biol. 202, 3295–3303. — silk membrane / anchor-fibre strength.
  5. DeJong, J. T. et al. (2010). Bio-mediated soil improvement. Ecol. Eng. 36, 197–210. — microbially induced calcite (MICP) bonding the membrane to raw rock.
  6. Zeitlin, C. et al. (2013). Measurements of energetic particle radiation in transit to Mars on the MSL. Science 340, 1080–1084. — surface GCR the regolith over-blanket must attenuate.
  7. Durante, M. & Cucinotta, F. A. (2011). Physical basis of radiation protection in space travel. Rev. Mod. Phys. 83, 1245–1281. — areal-density shielding by regolith.
  8. Anderson, M. S. et al. (2018). Life Support Baseline Values and Assumptions Document. NASA/TP-2015-218570/REV1. — O₂ at 0.84 kg·person⁻¹·day⁻¹ (the ~4,600 person-years from ~1,400 t).
